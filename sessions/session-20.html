Lesson 10 of 10 — Block 3: Advanced Relationships

Association, Aggregation & Composition

Inheritance ("is-a") is only one way objects relate. Far more common in real systems is "has-a" and "uses-a": a Teacher teaches in a Classroom, a Classroom has Students, a Classroom contains Desks that are destroyed when the room is demolished. This lesson gives you the vocabulary — and the Java patterns — for connecting objects correctly.

1. Beyond Inheritance — Why Relationships Matter

In Lessons 06–08 you mastered inheritance — the "is-a" relationship. A Book is a LibraryItem. But not every connection is inheritance. A Teacher is not a Classroom. A Desk is not a Student. These are has-a and uses-a connections — and getting them wrong leads to bugs that inheritance cannot fix.

Definition — Object Relationship

An object relationship exists when one class holds a reference to an object of another class (a field or property pointing at another object). The three standard types — association, aggregation, composition — differ in strength, ownership, and lifetime.

The "is-a" vs "has-a" Test

Before writing code, ask: "Is X a type of Y?" → inheritance. "Does X use/have/contain Y?" → association, aggregation, or composition. Never inherit just to reuse a field — that is a relationship mistake.
